# ASLG User Guide

**Al Saif Legal Group — Unified Legal System Portal**
Website address: **https://app.alsaiflegalgroup.com/**

This is the firm's single place of record. Instead of case details in one
system, documents in a shared folder, hearing dates in a diary and reminders in
a chat group, everything lives here: the cases, the documents attached to them,
the hearing dates, the tasks people owe each other, and the record of who did
what.

This guide explains how to use it **page by page, in the same order the pages
appear in the menu**. It is written for everyday users, not for technical staff.
No programming knowledge is needed.

If you read only one part, read **Part 1 — Before you start**. It covers signing
in, moving around, and the small rules (like the red star on forms) that apply
everywhere.

# Part 1 — Before you start

## 1.1 Signing in

1. Open **https://app.alsaiflegalgroup.com/** in your browser.
2. The **Sign In** screen appears, headed *Unified Legal System Portal*.
3. Type your **Username** and **Password**.
4. Press **Sign In**.

If the details are wrong you will see **"Invalid username or password"**. The
message is deliberately vague — it does not tell you which of the two was wrong.

Every sign-in attempt, successful or failed, is recorded in the **Activity Log**
along with the IP address it came from.

**You cannot create your own account.** Accounts are created by a System Admin
on the **Users & Permissions** page. If you cannot sign in, contact your System
Admin — they can reset your password for you.

---

## 1.2 The screen: menu, title bar, bell

**On a computer**, the menu is the dark column down the side. It lists only the
pages your role is allowed to open, always in this order:

| Menu entry | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Dashboard** | Your daily summary and the hearings coming up |
| **Cases** | The firm's case list, case details, notes and stage — plus the **Official Search Engine** tab for looking up cases, sessions, experts and execution files |
| **Document Center** | Upload, preview and download case documents |
| **Notifications** | Every alert the system has sent you — plus the **Reminders & Follow-ups** tab for the tasks you owe other people and they owe you |
| **Users & Permissions** | Manage accounts *(System Admin only)* |
| **Activity Log** | The audit trail *(System Admin only)* |

If an entry is missing from your menu, your role does not have access to it.
That is normal, not a fault.

**On a phone**, the first four entries appear as buttons along the bottom of the
screen. The rest sit behind the **More** button (the "…" icon), together with
the language switch and **Logout**.

**The title bar** across the top shows the name of the page you are on, a globe
button (language), and a **bell**.

**The bell** shows a red number when you have unread notifications. Clicking it
takes you straight to the **Notifications** page. It does not open a preview
list — there is one place notifications are read, and the bell is a shortcut to
it.

---

## 1.3 The red star on forms

Everywhere in the system, a field label carrying a **red star (\*)** must be
filled in. Fields without a star are optional and can be left blank.

Under each form you will see the note **"\* Required field"** as a reminder.

If you press the save button while a starred field is empty, the system stops
and shows **"This field is required"** instead of saving. Nothing you already
typed is lost — fill in the missing field and press the button again.

---

## 1.4 Arabic and English

The system runs fully in both languages. Press the **globe** button in the title
bar (or **Switch to Arabic / التبديل إلى الإنجليزية** in the phone **More**
menu) to change language at any time.

Switching also flips the page direction — Arabic lays out right-to-left, English
left-to-right. Your choice is remembered on that device, so the system opens in
the same language next time.

Switching language does not change any data. It only changes what you see. Case
parties, summaries and categories are stored separately in Arabic and English;
where the English version of a particular text was never filled in, the Arabic
text is shown instead so nothing appears blank.

---

## 1.5 Dates and times

**Every date and time in the system is Kuwait local time (UTC+3).**

Times are shown in 12-hour format with **AM** or **PM** — for example:

- **9:30 AM**
- **10:00 AM**
- **11:00 AM**

A full timestamp looks like `09 Sep 2026, 09:30 AM`. Morning times carry **AM**,
afternoon and evening times carry **PM**. Kuwait does not use daylight saving,
so the time you see never shifts by an hour at any point in the year.

**"Today" means today in Kuwait.** Anywhere the system talks about a day —
*Today's Hearings* on the Dashboard, searching for hearings on a particular
**Session Date** — it uses the Kuwait calendar day, running from 12:00 AM to
11:59 PM Kuwait time. A hearing listed for a date stays on that date no matter
where the person looking at it happens to be.

This applies everywhere without exception: hearing times, task due dates,
notification timestamps, document upload times, and the Activity Log. When you
type a date into a form (a hearing date, a task due date), type it as Kuwait
local time — that is exactly how it will be read back to you.

### The working week

The system uses the firm's own working week, not a Western one:

| Day | |
|---|---|
| **Sunday** | Working day — **the first day of the business week** |
| **Monday** | Working day |
| **Tuesday** | Working day |
| **Wednesday** | Working day |
| **Thursday** | Working day |
| **Friday** | **Weekend — the only weekend day** |
| **Saturday** | Working day |

This matters in one specific place: when the system works out **when to send
the three warnings** before a hearing (see
[3.1](#31-the-three-warnings-before-every-deadline)), it will not leave a
warning sitting on a **Friday** — it moves that warning **earlier**, to the
Thursday. **Saturday is treated as a normal working day** and a warning landing
on a Saturday is left exactly where it is.

Nothing else in the system blocks or hides anything at the weekend: you can
sign in, upload, search and update a case on a Friday like any other day.

## 1.6 Signing out

Press **Logout** at the bottom of the side menu, or **Logout** in the phone
**More** menu.

Always sign out on a shared or public computer. Closing the browser tab is not
the same as signing out.

## 1.7 Printing

Several pages have a **Print** button. It prints **only the information on that
page** — never the menus, the buttons, the search boxes or whatever else is on
your screen.

What comes out is a clean sheet with:

- the firm's name at the top,
- **the date and time you printed it**, and **who printed it**,
- the record itself, laid out as a table or a list,
- a footer confirming that every time on the page is **Kuwait local time,
  12-hour AM/PM**.

It follows whichever language you are using: print in Arabic and you get a
right-to-left Arabic sheet, print in English and you get an English one.

**Where to find it**

| Page | Button prints |
|---|---|
| Dashboard | The eight figures, then the Critical Upcoming Hearings list |
| Cases → Official Search Engine tab | The results of the search you just ran, with the criteria you used |
| Cases | The case list exactly as you have filtered it |
| Cases → **Case details** | The full case record: number, court, lawyer, stage, parties, summary, timeline and every note |
| Document Center | The document register: which case, file name, size, review status, upload date |
| Notifications → Reminders & Follow-ups tab | Your task list as filtered, with due dates, assignees and status |
| Notifications | Your own notification history |
| Activity Log | The audit trail *(System Admin only)* |

**Notes**

- On the **Official Search Engine** the Print button only appears **after** a
  search has returned results — there is nothing to print before that.
- If there is nothing on the page to print, the button tells you so rather than
  producing a blank page.
- The **Document Center** button prints the *register* — the list of documents.
  To print a document's own contents, open it with **Preview** and print it
  from there.
- There is deliberately **no Print button on Users & Permissions**. That page
  holds staff account details, and the firm has no routine need for a printed
  copy of it.

# Part 2 — The pages, in menu order

## 2.1 Dashboard

**Menu entry:** Dashboard — the page you land on after signing in.

The Dashboard answers one question: *what needs my attention today?*

### The eight boxes

**Every box is clickable.** Clicking one takes you to the page that lists those
exact records, already filtered — you never have to go and find them by hand.
Each box shows its own action wording at the bottom (for example *View cases*).
Boxes also respond to the keyboard: tab to one and press Enter or Space.

**Top row — your overall picture**

| Box | The number is | Clicking it opens |
|---|---|---|
| **Unread Notifications** | Alerts you have not opened | **Notifications** |
| **Pending Documents** | Documents still waiting to be reviewed. Review one and this number drops — see [Reviewing a document](#reviewing-a-document--approve-and-reject) | **Document Center**, filtered to pending |
| **Today's Hearings** | Hearings scheduled for today | **Cases**, filtered to today's hearings |
| **Active Cases** | Cases that are not closed | **Cases**, filtered to active |

**Second row — Tasks & Assignments**

This row appears only if your role has access to Reminders & Follow-ups.

| Box | The number is | Clicking it opens |
|---|---|---|
| **My Open Tasks** | Tasks assigned to you, not yet finished | **Reminders & Follow-ups**, filtered to your open tasks |
| **Overdue Tasks** | Your open tasks whose due date has passed | **Reminders & Follow-ups**, filtered to overdue |
| **Pending Status Requests** | People waiting for *you* to say where a case stands | **Reminders & Follow-ups**, filtered to status requests |
| **Tasks I Assigned** | Tasks you gave to other people, still open | **Reminders & Follow-ups**, filtered to what you assigned |

When you arrive from a box, a grey **filter chip** appears at the top of the
page naming the filter. Press the **×** on the chip to clear it and see
everything.

**If Overdue Tasks is not zero, start there.** The system is designed so that
every deadline is announced *before* it arrives (see
[Part 3.1](#31-the-three-warnings-before-every-deadline)) — a number in this box
means a warning was already sent and the deadline still passed.

### Critical Upcoming Hearings

Below the boxes is the **Critical Upcoming Hearings** panel: the next hearings
in date order, each with a **live countdown** in **days, hours and minutes**,
updating while you watch. The countdown turns red once a hearing is less than a
day away. A hearing that has already taken place shows the **Completed** badge
instead of a countdown.

Each hearing row carries up to two buttons:

- **Open Case** — opens that case's details immediately.
- **Request Update** — asks a colleague for a status update on that case
  (System Admin and Lawyer only). It is the same dialog described under
  [Request Update](#request-update) and behaves identically wherever it appears.

If there is nothing urgent, the panel says **"No urgent hearings"**.

**Printing.** **Print** at the top of the page produces a one-page briefing: the eight figures, then the Critical Upcoming Hearings list with each hearing's Kuwait date and time. See [1.7](#17-printing).

### Useful Kuwait Websites

Above Critical Upcoming Hearings is the **Useful Kuwait Websites** panel —
direct links to official Kuwaiti authorities, shown as buttons in the same
style as the Quick Actions row. Hover over one to see what the site offers.
They open in a new tab, so you do not lose your place.

| Website | Address | What it offers | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ministry of Justice** | www.moj.gov.kw | Cases, hearings, real-estate registration, lawyer services | Follow up on cases and complete judicial transactions |
| **E-Services of MOJ** | eservices.moj.gov.kw | Issuing and cancelling powers of attorney, verifying validity | Handle power-of-attorney procedures electronically |
| **Public Authority for Civil Information** | www.paci.gov.kw | Civil ID data | Verify a client's identity |
| **Civil Service Commission** | www.csc.gov.kw | Regulations and government jobs | Administrative and legal reference |

These are external government sites run by the Kuwaiti authorities, not part of
this system. ASLG does not control their availability or their content.

### Quick Actions, Action Stream, and the other new Dashboard widgets

Below the boxes, the Dashboard shows, from top to bottom: **My Week** and
**Watched Cases** side by side, then **Useful Kuwait Websites**, then
**Critical Upcoming Hearings**, then the **Action Stream** — each across the
full width of the page. On a phone, My Week and Watched Cases stack one
above the other instead; nothing is hidden.

**Quick Actions** — a row of buttons, right below the page title. You only
ever see the ones your role can use:

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **New Case** | Takes you to Cases, ready to press New Case |
| **Assign a Task** | Takes you to Reminders & Follow-ups |
| **Mark All Notifications Read** | Marks every notification read, right there — no page change |
| **Request Case Update** | Pick a case, then send the usual Request Update ask |
| **Check Official Portal** | Opens Cases on its Official Search Engine tab |
| **Sync Deadlines Now** | Refreshes the Deadlines figures immediately |

**Quick search (Ctrl+K)** — a button beside the page title (or the Ctrl+K
keyboard shortcut while the Dashboard is open) opens a small search box.
Type to jump straight to any page you have access to, any of the Quick
Actions above, or any case by number or party name — press Enter or click a
result to go there.

**Action Stream.** One combined, most-urgent-first list of your notifications
and your open tasks, so you don't have to check two places. Overdue tasks
show first, then tasks due within a few days, then everything else with the
newest at the top. Click a task that names a case to open that case directly.

**My Week.** How many tasks are due in the next 7 days, how many you've
finished in the last 7 days, and a small progress bar. Only shown if your
role uses Reminders & Follow-ups.

**Watched Cases.** Every case you are currently watching (the same bookmark
as **Watch Case** in Case Details or **Track** on the Official Search
Engine), with its next hearing time if one is scheduled. Click one to open
it.

## 2.2 Cases

**Menu entry:** Cases. This one page has two tabs across the top:

| Tab | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Cases** | The firm's case list, case details, notes and stage — described in this section |
| **Official Search Engine** | Search by case number, sessions, experts, execution files, or across the firm's records — see [2.3](#23-cases--official-search-engine-tab) |

**Clients see only Cases** — the Official Search Engine tab is not shown to
them at all. Switching between the two tabs keeps your place in each: a search
you ran is still there when you come back to it.

**To refresh a tab, click it** — even if you are already on it. It reloads that
tab from the server (a small spinner shows on the tab while it does) without
clearing what you typed: on Official Search Engine, your last search runs again.
You rarely need it: the list already refreshes itself after anything you change,
and is at most a minute behind changes colleagues make.

Old bookmarks to the Official Search Engine still work — they open this page on
the Official Search Engine tab.

### The Cases tab

Page heading: *Cases & Case Statements*. The case list, with two dropdown
filters above it:

- **Status** — All / Active / Closed.
- **Assigned Lawyer** — All, or one specific lawyer.

If you arrived from a Dashboard box, the matching filter is already applied and
a filter chip is shown at the top; press the **×** on the chip to clear it.

The table lists **Case Number**, **Court Level/Category**, **Assigned Lawyer**
and **Status**. Click any row to open the case.

| Field | Required | Notes |

| **Year** \* | Yes | |
| **Court** \* | Yes | Pick from *Select Court* |
| **Parties (Arabic)** \* | Yes | |
| Category (Arabic) / Category (English) | No | |

### Case details

Clicking a case opens **Case Details**, containing:

- **Badges** across the top: case number/year, court, assigned lawyer.
- **Watch Case / Watching** — the bookmark toggle. This is the same relationship
  as **Track** on the Official Search Engine: while it reads **Watching**, you
  receive the pre-hearing alerts for this case.
- **The case summary.**
- **Case Timeline** — see below.
- **Status** — a dropdown to change the case stage (only if your role may edit).
- **Notes** — the discussion thread.
- **Schedule Reminder** — create a task on this case (only if your role may).
- **Close** — closes the window.

### Case Timeline — what it does and does not do

The **Case Timeline** is a **historical record of milestones**, listed oldest to
newest, each with its date. Completed steps are marked as done.

**Be clear about how it is filled in.** This matters, because it is easy to
assume otherwise:

- A step is added **automatically when the case is first created** — *Case
  Filed* (تسجيل الدعوى), dated the moment of creation.
- Any further steps in a timeline came from data that was loaded into the
  system.
- **Changing the case stage does NOT add a timeline step.** The stage dropdown
  and the timeline are two different things. Changing the stage from
  *Preparation* to *Pleading* updates the stage badge everywhere and writes an
  Activity Log entry — but the timeline is unchanged.
- **Changing a hearing date does not rewrite the timeline either.** Past
  timeline entries are history; they stay exactly as they were recorded.
- **Request Update does not add a timeline step.** It creates a task for a
  person. It never invents a new progress point on the case.
- **There is no button anywhere in the application to add a timeline step by
  hand.**

So if a case's timeline shows only *Case Filed*, that is correct and expected —
it is not a fault and nothing is missing. To record what is happening on a case,
use **Notes** (which anyone with edit rights can add at any time) and the
**Status** dropdown.

If a case has no steps at all, the timeline says **"No progress steps have been
recorded for this case yet."**

**Request Update on the timeline.** System Admins and Lawyers see a **Request
Update** button on the **last (most recent)** timeline step — the point where
progress has stopped, which is where asking "where are we?" makes sense. It
opens the same dialog described under [Request Update](#request-update) and
behaves identically: it creates a task for a person. **It does not add a
timeline step and it does not advance the case.**

### Case status (stage)

A case moves through these stages:

| Stage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **New** | Just registered |
| **Preparation** | Being prepared |
| **Pleading** | In pleadings before the court |
| **Judgment Issued** | Judgment has been handed down |
| **Execution** | Judgment being enforced |
| **Closed** | Finished |

To change it, open the case and pick the new value under **Status**. The change
is recorded in the **Activity Log** with your name.

Setting a case to **Closed** also stops its automatic hearing alerts — the
system will not chase people about a hearing on a case that is over.

### Notes

Notes are the case discussion thread. Type in the **Add Note** box and press
**Add Note**. Each note is permanently stamped with your name and the date and
time.

Notes cannot be edited or deleted after posting, and every note is recorded in
the Activity Log. Write them as you would a file note: factual, dated,
professional.

Use notes for anything worth remembering: what the court said, what a client
asked for, what you agreed to do next.

**Printing.** **Print** at the top of the Cases page prints the list exactly as you have filtered it, naming the filters at the top of the sheet. Inside **Case details**, **Print** produces the full case record — number, court, lawyer, stage, parties, summary, the whole timeline and every note. See [1.7](#17-printing).

### User ↔ Case Linking (user_case_links)

**Purpose.** An explicit, Admin/owning-Lawyer-controlled link between a Client
user and a case. This is **additive** to the existing Civil ID match
(`cases.civil_id` = `users.civil_id`) — that mechanism keeps working exactly as
before. A link is for the case where the client's Civil ID isn't on file, or
where a case's `civil_id` column is being used to record the *opposing*
party's ID rather than the firm's own client's. One user can be linked to many
cases, and one case can carry many linked users (co-clients, power of
attorney).

**Where to manage it.** Open **Case Details** → **Linked Clients**. Visible
only to a **System Admin** (any case) or the case's own **assigned Lawyer**
(their case only) — nobody else sees this block. It lists every actively
linked client (name, who linked them and when, and whether they hold upload
access), each with an **Unlink** button, plus a **Link a Client** control to
add another (pick a Client-role user, and optionally tick **Allow document
upload**).

**Storage.** A dedicated table, `user_case_links`, with a `status` of
`active`/`revoked` — unlinking **revokes** the row rather than deleting it, so
who-linked-whom-when stays in the record. A database-level functional unique
key guarantees at most one `active` row can ever exist for the same
(user, case) pair — re-linking the same pair revokes the old row and inserts a
fresh one, and this holds even under a race between two admins acting at once.

**Upload access — two coexisting paths.** A linked client with **Allow
document upload** ticked gets a **standing** upload permission for that case —
no expiry unless a `can_upload_until` timestamp is set (reserved for a future
expiring-access UI; nothing sets it today). This exists **alongside**, not
instead of, the older **Grant Temporary Upload Access** one-time window (see
above) — a client can hold both at once, on the same or different cases, and
the Document Center shows one upload box per active source, each honestly
labeled ("Standing upload access" vs. "Expires at …").

**Notifications.** A linked client (or a civil_id-matched one) is notified
when the case's **stage changes** (old stage → new stage, plus the case
number) and when a **note is added** — the note's own text is never included,
only the fact that one exists. Recipients are de-duplicated: someone connected
both by civil_id and by an explicit link still gets exactly one notification
per event, never two.

**Migration.** The `user_case_links` table is not created by the running
application — its database account has no CREATE/ALTER/DROP privilege by
design. It's applied once, by hand, from `db/migration_user_case_links.sql`
(requires MySQL 8.0.13+ for the functional unique index used above). Rollback
is a plain `DROP TABLE user_case_links;`, safe only once the corresponding
application code has also been reverted.

## 2.3 Cases → Official Search Engine tab

**Where:** the **Cases** menu entry, second tab. Heading: *Integrated Official
Search Engine*, subtitle *Matches Kuwait Ministry of Justice e-Services*.

This tab has **five search tabs of its own** across the top.

| Tab | Use it to find | Fields you can search on |
|---|---|---|
| **Case Number Search** | A case by its official number | Court Level/Category, Case Number, Year, Civil ID / Party Name |
| **Sessions & Circuits** | Court sessions and the circuit they sit in | Circuit, Session Date |
| **Experts** | Court-appointed expert files | Expert File No., Expert Name |
| **Execution** | Enforcement / execution files | Execution File No., Judgment Enforcement Status |
| **Internal Firm Search** | Anything inside the firm's own records | One box: *Search firm cases and documents…* |

Fill in what you know and press **Search**, or press **Reset** to clear the form
and start again. Before you search, the page shows the hint **"Enter search
criteria then press Search"**. If nothing matches you get **"No matching
records. Try adjusting your search criteria."**

### Reading a result

You do not need to memorise case numbers. **Every result on the Sessions &
Circuits, Experts and Execution tabs shows the case it belongs to**: the case
number and year, the current stage badge, the parties, and the court. If that
case has been closed, a **Closed** badge is shown as well.

This information is read from the case record itself every time you search, so a
result can never disagree with what the Cases page says. If a colleague changes
a case's stage, your next search shows the new stage.

**Case Number Search puts the exact match first.** If you search for case number
`1123`, the case actually numbered 1123 appears at the top, above any partial
matches.

**You only ever see what you are allowed to see.** Search results obey the same
permission rules as the rest of the system. A client (**Client** role) searching
sees only their own cases and documents; they never see another client's records
in a search result.

### Actions on every result

Each result row carries the same set of buttons.

#### Open Case

Opens the full case details for the case behind that result — the same view you
get from the **Cases** page.

#### Track

**Track** adds the case to your personal tracked list.

- Before you track it, the button reads **Track**, tooltip *"Add this case to
  your tracked list"*.
- After you track it, the button changes colour and reads **Tracked**, tooltip
  *"You are tracking this case — click to stop tracking"*, and you see the
  message *"Case added to your tracked list"*.
- Pressing it again untracks the case and shows *"Stopped tracking this case"*.

The state is remembered. If you search for the same case tomorrow, the button
still reads **Tracked**. Inside the case details, the same relationship appears
as the **Watching** bookmark.

**Tracking is personal.** It does not assign anything to anyone and it does not
change the case. What it does do is put you on the notification list: **you
receive the pre-hearing warnings for every case you track**, exactly as the
assigned lawyer does. That is the reason to use it.

The older wording **Import to Tracking List** refers to the same action.

#### Request Update

*(System Admin and Lawyer only.)* Use this to ask a specific colleague where a
case stands.

1. Press **Request Update**. A dialog opens showing **About** — the case you are
   asking about.
2. **Ask** — choose the colleague to ask.
3. **Reply needed by** \* — the date and time you need an answer by. Required,
   and it must be in the future.
4. **Message (optional)** — say what you need, for example *"Has the memo been
   filed? Please update the status."*
5. Press **Send Request**.

You will see **"Update request sent successfully"**.

What actually happens: a real task is created in **Reminders & Follow-ups**,
assigned to the person you chose, and they are notified. The dialog says this
plainly: *"The person you choose will be notified and will see this under
'Reminders & Follow-ups'. Asking for an update does not change the case status
by itself."*

**Requesting an update never changes the case.** The stage only changes when
someone actually changes it on the Cases page.

If the reply-by date is missing or in the past you are told
(*"Please choose a reply-by date"* / *"The reply-by date must be in the
future"*). If no colleague is available to ask, the dialog says *"There are no
staff members available to ask"*.

**Printing.** Once a search returns results, a **Print** button appears beside **Results**. It prints the criteria you searched on and the rows that came back — the current tab only. See [1.7](#17-printing).

## 2.4 Document Center

**Menu entry:** Document Center. Page heading: *Document Locker & Upload
Center*.

Documents are grouped **by case**. Each case is a row you can expand and
collapse (tooltip: *"Show/hide this case's documents"*), showing how many
documents it holds — *1 document*, or *5 documents*. A case with none shows

If you arrived by clicking the **Pending Documents** box on the Dashboard, only
pending documents are listed.

### Document status: Pending Review, Approved, Rejected

Every document carries a status badge next to its name:

| Badge | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Pending Review** (amber) | Uploaded, nobody has checked it yet |
| **Approved** (green) | Checked and accepted |
| **Rejected** (red) | Checked and not accepted — usually needs replacing |

Every new upload starts as **Pending Review**. The Dashboard's **Pending
Documents** box counts exactly these, so it is a live to-do list: review the
documents and the number comes down.

### Uploading (staff)

**A document always belongs to a case, so you upload it from the case.** There
is no general "upload anywhere" box.

1. Find the case in the list and expand it.
2. Press the **+** button on the case row (*"Add a document to this case"*), or
   **Add New Document** if the case has no documents yet.
3. A window opens with an upload area. **Drag and drop your files onto it, or
   click it to browse.**

- **Accepted types:** PDF, DOCX, JPG, PNG
- **Maximum size:** 25 MB per file

A progress bar appears while each file uploads. If a file is the wrong type or
too big, that file is refused with **"Unsupported file type or exceeds size
limit"** — the rest of your files still upload.

Clients upload differently — see
[Grant Temporary Upload Access](#grant-temporary-upload-access) below.

### Reviewing a document — Approve and Reject

**Who can do this: System Admin and Lawyer only.** Consultants and Delegates can
upload and delete documents but cannot sign one off as reviewed — that is the
case owner's decision. Clients cannot review anything.

If you are allowed to review, two extra buttons appear on each document row:

- **Approve** (tick) — accept the document.
- **Reject** (cross) — do not accept it. A small window opens where you can type
  an optional **Reason for rejection** (for example *"The scan is unreadable,
  please upload it again"*), then press **Reject**.

You will see **"Document approved"** or **"Document rejected"**, and the badge
changes immediately.

**What happens next:**

- The status changes everywhere at once — the badge on the row, and the
  **Pending Documents** count on the Dashboard.
- **The person who uploaded the document is notified privately**, and the
  rejection reason is included in that message. This matters most for clients:
  someone who sent you a document through a temporary upload window finds out
  the outcome without having to ask.
- The action is recorded in the **Activity Log** with your name and, for a
  rejection, the reason.

**Things worth knowing:**

- You only ever see the button that would actually change something. An
  already-approved document shows only **Reject**, and vice versa — so one
  decision can never send two notifications.
- Pressing Approve on a document someone else already approved does nothing and
  notifies nobody. Two people reviewing the same row cannot produce a duplicate.
- Reviewing your own upload notifies nobody — you already know.
- A decision can be changed: an approved document can later be rejected, and a
  rejected one approved. Each real change notifies the uploader once.
- **A document cannot be put back to "Pending Review" once reviewed.** If you
  need it looked at again, ask for a fresh copy to be uploaded.

### Preview, download, delete

Each document row shows the **File Name**, **Size**, **Uploaded By** and
**Uploaded At**, with buttons:

- **Preview** (eye icon) — view it in the browser, with a **Download** link.
- **Delete** (bin icon) — shown only if your role may delete.

Previews, downloads and deletions are all recorded in the Activity Log,
including who did it.

### Grant Temporary Upload Access

*(Staff roles only.)* By default a client (**Client** role) **cannot** upload
documents. They see: *"You can't upload documents right now. Ask your lawyer to
grant you temporary access to this case."*

To let a client send you a document:

1. Press **Grant Temporary Upload Access** (top right).
2. Choose the **Linked Case** \*.
3. Choose the **Duration** \* — 15 minutes, 30 minutes, 60 minutes, 4 hours, or
   24 hours.
4. Press **Grant Temporary Upload Access**.

The client then sees a green banner reading **"You have upload access"** with
the case name and an **Expires at** time, and an upload box appears beneath it.
This banner-and-box is the **only** upload route a client ever has.

The window closes itself **the moment they successfully upload one file**, even
if time was left on the clock. If they never upload anything, it simply expires.
Either way access reverts to blocked automatically — there is nothing to switch
off afterwards, and a client can never be left with a standing ability to
upload.

This is deliberately narrow: one case, one file, a time limit, and every grant
and upload recorded in the Activity Log.

**Printing.** **Print** at the top of the page prints the document *register*: which case each file belongs to, its name, size, review status and upload date. It does not print the files themselves — for that, open one with **Preview** and print it from there. See [1.7](#17-printing).

## 2.8 Activity Log

**Menu entry:** Activity Log (shown as **Log** on a phone). Page heading:
*System Activity & Audit Log*. **System Admin only** — other roles see *"This
page is restricted to system administrators"*.

A permanent record of who did what and when, with the columns **Time**,
**User**, **Action**, **Type**, **Reference** and **IP Address**.

Recorded actions include: Login, Failed login attempt, Logout, User created,
User updated, User deleted, Password reset, Bulk user activation, Bulk user
deactivation, Bulk password reset, Document uploaded, **Document approved**,
**Document rejected**, Document deleted, Document downloaded/previewed, Granted
temporary upload access, Case created, Case stage updated, Case note added,
**Reminder created**, **Reminder resolved**, **Case tracked**, **Case
untracked**, **Case watch added**, **Case watch removed**, status-update
requests, and the database backup, restore and reset tools.

**Following and tracking a case are recorded separately**, because they are two
different buttons:

| Entry | Where the person pressed it | Also records |
|---|---|---|
| **Case tracked** / **Case untracked** | The **Track** button on an Official Search Engine result | Which search result it came from |
| **Case watch added** / **Case watch removed** | The **Watch Case** control inside **Case details** | Whether the follow originally came from a search import or was added by hand |

Both end up following the same case; the entry tells you which route was used.

**Reminder entries** name the case, the task type, who it was assigned to and
when it was due. A **Reminder resolved** entry also records whether it was
marked *Done* or *Dismissed* and — importantly — whether resolving it moved the
case to a new stage, so a stage change never appears in the log without an
explanation beside it.

**Only things that actually happened appear here.** An entry is written *after*
the change has been saved, never before, so an attempt that was refused —
because the person lacked permission, or the record did not exist, or the
request was malformed — leaves no entry claiming it succeeded. If you see an
action in this log, it took place.

**Repeating an action that changes nothing does not add a second entry.**
Pressing **Watch Case** on a case you already follow, **Track** removal on a
case you are not tracking, or **Dismiss** on a task already closed all record
nothing, because nothing happened. Pressing **Track** twice *does* record
twice — each press genuinely files another import — and the second entry marks
itself as a repeat.

**What is deliberately not recorded.** Opening the **Notifications** page,
clicking a notification to mark it read, and **Mark all as read** are not
logged. They are a person reading their own inbox rather than acting on a case,
a document or an account, and recording them would add an entry every time
someone glanced at their alerts without answering any question this log exists
to answer. This is a deliberate choice, not an omission.

**Export CSV** downloads the log for offline review or archiving.

**About the IP Address column.** It records the **public IP address of the
device the person signed in from** — the address their internet connection
presents to the outside world, not an address belonging to this server. It is
recovered through Cloudflare, which is the only thing that can see the original
visitor, and it is only believed when the request genuinely arrived through the
firm's own Cloudflare tunnel; see
[4.3](#43-what-the-activity-logs-ip-address-column-can-and-cannot-tell-you).

**Every action performed through the application records an address** — signing
in, a failed sign-in, signing out, creating a case, adding a note, changing a
stage, uploading, approving, rejecting, downloading or deleting a document,
granting upload access, asking for a status update, creating and resolving a
reminder, tracking, untracking, watching and unwatching a case, and every user
and database administration action.

You will not find the address handled differently from one action to the next.
It is worked out **once, when the request arrives**, and attached to whatever
that request goes on to record — so a new kind of action is covered the moment
it is added, rather than needing to be wired up separately.

Three things to know when reading the column:

- **A blank address on an old entry is normal.** Address recording was extended
  to every action later than the log itself; entries written before that show a
  blank. Nothing was gone back and filled in — audit records are never
  rewritten.
- **Old sign-in entries show `[::1]:` followed by a number.** That is not
  anyone's address: it is this server talking to itself, which is all the
  application could see before this was corrected. Those rows are also left
  exactly as written.
- **A blank address on a new entry means there genuinely was no visitor** — an
  action carried out by the system itself rather than by a person at a browser.
  The server's own address is never written in as a stand-in.

The log is append-only: entries are never edited or removed from within the
application, and no password is ever written into it. Failed login attempts are
recorded with their IP address — a burst of them from one address is worth
investigating.

**Printing.** **Print** produces a paper copy of the audit trail. **Export CSV** remains the machine-readable route; **Print** is the one an auditor signs and files. See [1.7](#17-printing).

## 2.9 Procedural Intelligence — Deadlines & the Procedure tab

**Not yet active on this server** — this feature is built and tested but its database changes have not been applied to the live database yet. Everything below describes it so you know what to expect once it is switched on; until then you won't see it.

Once active, this feature answers one question for any case: **what actually happened last, what's required next, who's responsible for it, and by when?**

### Where you'll find it

- **Case Details → Procedural Intelligence.** Opening any case now shows its latest recorded event, any deadlines the system has worked out from it, and a **Record Procedure** button to log the next real thing that happens (a hearing, a judgment, a filing, anything). A full history of every event on the case sits below, collapsed by default.
- **Deadlines** (new sidebar entry). Every deadline across every case you can see, in one list — overdue ones in red, with **Confirm** and **Waive** buttons for the ones you can act on.
- **Cases → Official Search Engine tab → Check Official Portal.** A small panel under the search tabs: pick a case and a real government source (MOJ e-Services, Sahel, etc.), click **Open official portal** to sign in yourself in a new tab, then come back and record what you found. Nothing you see on the five search tabs above it is live government data — it never has been, it's the firm's own records styled to look familiar — and this panel is the honest, human-in-the-loop way to actually check the real thing.
- **Procedure Rules** (new sidebar entry, System Admin and Lawyer only). The list of legal deadline rules the system knows about — each with its legal citation and whether it's been switched on.

### The most important thing to understand: a "Suggested" badge is not a fact

Every deadline you see carries one of two badges:

- **Confirmed** (green) — a lawyer has verified the legal rule behind this date against the actual law. You'll get the normal three-warning countdown for it (see [3.1](#31-the-three-warnings-before-every-deadline)).
- **Suggested** (amber) — the system computed this date from a rule nobody has verified yet. It's a starting point, not something to file a deadline by without checking. A Client account never sees a Suggested item at all — only Confirmed ones on their own case.

If a case's latest event has no rule at all behind it, the Procedure tab says so plainly ("No verified rule covers this step") instead of guessing a date. That's deliberate: this system is built to never let an AI-computed guess look like a legal fact.

### Turning a rule on (System Admin, or a firm-owner Lawyer, only)

On the **Procedure Rules** page, an unverified rule can only be switched on after you've checked its cited law yourself and ticked a box confirming you did. This is the one action in this whole feature that asserts a real legal fact, so it's deliberately made to take a moment of real attention rather than a single careless click.

# Part 3 — How the system alerts you

## 3.1 The three warnings before every deadline

The firm's rule is simple: **no one should ever learn about a deadline after it
has passed.** The alerting is built around that rule.

For every scheduled hearing and every open task you receive **three warnings,
always before the date, always in the same order**:

| Warning | Wording you will see |
|---|---|
| **1 of 3** | *Upcoming hearing — early notice* / *Task due soon — early notice* |
| **2 of 3** | *Hearing approaching — action required* / *Task due — action required* |
| **3 of 3** | *Hearing tomorrow — final alert* / *Task due tomorrow — final alert* |

Each message names the case and the exact date and time.

**When they arrive.** When there is comfortable notice, the three warnings land
roughly **7 days, 3 days and 1 day** before the date. If something is scheduled
at short notice — a hearing set for the day after tomorrow — the three warnings
are **compressed into the time available** rather than skipped. You always get
all three.

**The weekend.** **Friday is the only weekend day** (see
[1.5 — The working week](#the-working-week)). If a warning would land on a
Friday it is moved **earlier**, to the Thursday — never later, because later
could push it past the very hearing it is warning about. **Saturday is a
working day** and a warning landing on a Saturday is left exactly where it is.

**They never arrive out of order.** Warning 1 always comes before warning 2,
which always comes before warning 3, and all three always come before the date
itself. This holds even when moving a warning off a Friday would otherwise make
two of them collide: order wins, and the alerts are delivered in sequence. You
will never receive "final alert" before "early notice".

**Nothing is sent after the date.** These are warnings, not post-mortems. If a
task deadline does pass, a separate and clearly worded *"Task deadline passed"*
message is sent — it reads as a report of what happened, not as an advance
warning.

**No duplicates.** Running the checks repeatedly does not resend anything. Each
warning is sent once per case, per date, per layer.

**Who receives hearing warnings:** the case's **assigned lawyer**, plus
**everyone tracking or watching that case**. Hearings that are cancelled,
completed, postponed, already in the past, or that belong to a **closed case**
are skipped entirely.

**Who receives task warnings:** the person the task is **assigned to**.

## 3.2 Smart Follow-up

If a task you assigned to someone sits untouched, the system chases them for you
— so you do not have to.

- The first automatic nudge is sent **2 days** after the task was assigned, if
  nothing has happened.
- It repeats **every 2 days**.
- It stops after **3 nudges** — the system does not chase anyone indefinitely.
- It stops **immediately** the moment the assignee marks the task **Done** or
  **Dismissed**.

**The assignee** receives: *"Smart Follow-up (1): your assigned task has not
received an update yet — [task] (Case [number]). Please review it and provide a
status update."*

**The person who assigned it** receives a separate courtesy copy: *"System
follow-up (1): the assigned employee has not provided an update yet on '[task]'
(Case [number]). A reminder was sent automatically on your behalf."*

Nobody else is involved. Both messages carry the **Smart Follow-up** tag so it
is clear a person did not send them.

To stop being nudged, answer the task on the **Reminders & Follow-ups** tab of
the **Notifications** page.

---

## 3.3 Who can see your notifications

**Your notifications are yours.** Each notification is addressed either to one
named person or to a whole role, and you only ever see the ones addressed to
you.

- A notification addressed to **you** is visible to you alone. No other account
  can see it — not colleagues, not other lawyers.
- A notification addressed to a **role** is a general announcement and is
  visible to everyone holding that role.
- A **System Admin cannot browse other people's notification lists.** Admin
  rights cover user accounts and the Activity Log, not other people's inboxes.

This is not simply a matter of the screen hiding things. The system itself
checks who is asking, every single time, and refuses to hand over a notification
that is not yours.

**Where personal notifications come from:** hearing warnings for cases you are
assigned to or tracking; tasks assigned to you; status updates you requested;
and Smart Follow-up nudges on your own tasks. **General announcements** are the
occasional system message sent to a role rather than to an individual.

**A practical note:** a notification includes the case number and the deadline so
it is useful at a glance. Do not put anything you would not want quoted into the
**Notes** field of a task — the assignee sees that text inside their
notification.

# Part 4 — Roles and administration

## 4.1 Who can do what

There are five roles. Your role decides which menu entries you see and what you
can do on each page.

Access levels used below: **full** (everything) · **edit** (view and change) ·
**view** (read only) · **limited** (a reduced view) · **own** (only your own
records) · **none** (no access, the menu entry is hidden).

| Module | System Admin | Lawyer | Consultant | Delegate | Client |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dashboard | full | full | full | full | limited |
| Official Search Engine | full | full | edit | edit | **none** |
| Cases | full | full | limited | view | own |
| Document Center | full | full | edit | edit | own |
| — of which: approve/reject a document | **yes** | **yes** | no | no | no |
| Notifications | full | full | full | full | full |
| Users & Permissions | full | **none** | **none** | **none** | **none** |
| Reminders & Follow-ups | full | full | edit | view | **none** |
| Activity Log | full | **none** | **none** | **none** | **none** |
| Deadlines *(Procedural Intelligence)* | full | full | edit | view | own |
| Procedure tab on a case *(Procedural Intelligence)* | full | full | edit | view | own |
| Check Official Portal *(Procedural Intelligence)* | full | full | edit | edit | **none** |
| Procedure Rules *(Procedural Intelligence)* | full | view | **none** | **none** | **none** |

In plain terms:

- **System Admin** — the IT administrator. Full access everywhere, including
  Users & Permissions, the Activity Log, and the System Maintenance tools.
- **Lawyer** — full access to legal work: cases, documents, search, tasks. No

  upload documents unless a staff member grants them **Grant Temporary Upload
  Access** for a specific case and a limited time.

**Firm owner flag.** Independently of role, an account can be flagged as **Firm
owner**. Owners (and System Admins) may assign a task to any staff member;
everyone else can only assign to the case's own lawyer.

Every one of these limits is checked twice: once in what you are shown (so the
screen is not confusing), and again by the system itself every time anything is
requested. Someone who tried to go around the screen would still be refused.

## 4.2 Backup, Restore and Factory Reset

| Tool | Risk | What it touches |
|---|---|---|
| **Reset Client / Clear Local App Data** | Safe | Only this browser, on this device |
| **Full Backup** | Safe | Reads data out; changes nothing |
| **Import / Restore Backup** | ⚠ Destructive | Replaces the entire database and all documents |
| **Factory Reset / Reset Database to Seed Data** | ⚠⚠ Destructive and irreversible | Deletes everything, permanently |

---

### Reset Client / Clear Local App Data — safe

**What it does:** clears this browser's local storage on this device (your saved
session and locally cached preferences) and reloads the application.

**What it does not do:** it changes **nothing** on the server. All cases,
documents, users and settings remain exactly as they are, for everyone including
you.

**When to use it:** the interface is behaving oddly — stale content, a page not
refreshing, a display glitch. This is the first thing to try.

**What you will see:** a confirmation explaining *"You'll be signed out and the
app will reload fresh on this device only. Nothing on the server is affected."*
Press **Reset This Device Now**. You will be signed out and can sign straight
back in.

---

### Full Backup — safe

**What it does:** produces one compressed **ZIP** file containing a full
database export (users, cases, permissions, activity log) **plus every uploaded
document file**, and downloads it immediately.

**What it does not do:** it reads data only. Nothing is altered or removed.

**When to use it:** on a regular schedule, and **always immediately before**
using Import / Restore or Factory Reset.

**What you will see:** *"Preparing backup…"* while it is built, then *"Backup
downloaded successfully"*. With many documents, expect it to take a while.

**Store the file securely.** It contains the firm's complete data. Keep it
encrypted, off the server, and treat it with the same care as the case files
themselves.

---

### Import / Restore Backup — ⚠ destructive

**What it does:** replaces the **current database and all uploaded documents**
with the contents of a backup ZIP previously produced by **Full Backup**.

**Everything currently in the system is replaced.** Any case, document, note or
user created since that backup was taken is gone.

**Safety measures built in:**

- A **safety backup of the current state is taken automatically** before
  anything is replaced.
- Only a ZIP produced by this system's **Full Backup** is accepted; the file is
  validated before it is used.
- You must type a confirmation phrase exactly.

**How to run it:**

1. Take a **Full Backup** first, so you can undo the undo.
2. Press **Import / Restore Backup**.
3. Read the warning: *"All current data will be replaced with the contents of
   the file you upload. A backup of the current state is taken automatically
   before proceeding, but make sure the uploaded file is the correct one
   first."*
4. **Choose backup file (ZIP)** — select the file, and check the date on it. If
   you press confirm without choosing one you are told *"Please choose a ZIP
   file first"*.
5. Type the confirmation phrase **exactly** as shown. A mismatch gives
   *"Confirmation phrase does not match"* and nothing happens.
6. Press **Replace Data & Restore**. You will see *"Restoring…"*, then
   *"Restored from backup successfully. You will now be signed out."*

Everyone is signed out. Sign back in with the credentials that were valid **in
the restored backup** — if the backup predates a password change, the old
password is the one that works.

**Verification note.** The restore feature, its confirmation phrase, the
automatic safety backup, the backup-file validation and the sign-out behaviour
are all present in the deployed application. A full end-to-end restore has
**not** been executed against the production database, because doing so would
have destroyed real data and no separate test database was available. Treat your
first real restore as a serious operation and schedule it deliberately.

---

### Factory Reset / Reset Database to Seed Data — ⚠⚠ destructive and irreversible

**What it does:** permanently deletes **every case, document, user, reminder and
the current activity log**, then reinitialises the database with the original
demo/seed dataset.

**There is no undo.** Once this completes, the only way back is a backup you
took beforehand. With no backup, the data is gone.

**When to use it:** essentially never on a live system. It exists for preparing
a fresh installation or resetting a demonstration environment.

**How to run it:**

1. Take a **Full Backup** first. Confirm the file downloaded and opens.
2. Press **Factory Reset / Reset Database to Seed Data**.
3. Read the warning: *"All real data will be permanently deleted and cannot be
   recovered after this operation. Make sure you have a backup if you need to
   keep the current data."*
4. Type the confirmation phrase **exactly** as prompted. A mismatch gives
   *"Confirmation phrase does not match"* and nothing happens.
5. Press **Delete Everything & Reset**.
6. You see *"Database reset to seed data successfully. You will now be signed
   out."*

Afterwards the system contains only the seed dataset. Sign in with the seed
administrator credentials, then immediately recreate the real accounts and
change every password.

## 4.3 What the Activity Log's IP Address column can and cannot tell you

The address shown is the **public IP address of the device someone signed in
from**. Recovering it correctly is not automatic, because a request reaches this
system through several hops:

> the person's browser → **Cloudflare** → the firm's **Cloudflare tunnel**
> running on the server → **IIS** → the application

Only Cloudflare ever sees the original visitor. Every hop after that sees the
hop before it, which is why the column used to show `[::1]:` and a number: the
server was faithfully recording the only address it could see — its own.

**How it is worked out now.** Cloudflare passes the visitor's address along with
the request. That claim is only trusted when the request demonstrably came in
through the firm's own tunnel. If someone reaches the server by some other route
and *claims* to be at a particular address, the claim is discarded and the
address they actually connected from is recorded instead. The system will
under-report before it will name the wrong person.

**What this depends on.** The Cloudflare tunnel (the `Cloudflared` service on
the server) must keep running. If it is stopped and the site is later published
some other way, the column will fall back to whatever address the new route
exposes — it will not silently start showing wrong addresses, but it may show
less useful ones. Anyone changing how the site is published should re-check this
column afterwards.

**Which actions carry an address.** Anything a person does through the
application: signing in and failed sign-ins, signing out, case creation, notes
and stage changes, every document action, upload-access grants, status-update
requests, reminders created and resolved, cases tracked, untracked, watched and
unwatched, and all user and database administration. The address is worked out
once when the request arrives and attached to whatever that request records, so
new kinds of action are covered automatically rather than needing to be added
one at a time.

**When it is legitimately blank.** Only when there was no visitor to record —
work the system performs on its own rather than on behalf of someone at a
browser. In that case the column stays empty on purpose. The server's own
address is never substituted for a missing one, because that would look like a
real person and be worse than an honest blank.

**What it is not.** It is not a location, and it is not proof of identity. Home
and mobile connections change address regularly, and several people behind one
office connection share a single address. Treat it as one signal among several —
useful for spotting a burst of failed sign-ins from one place, not for accusing
an individual.

# Part 5 — Reference

## 5.1 Common problems

**I cannot sign in.**
Check your keyboard language and that Caps Lock is off. If it still fails, ask a
System Admin to reset your password on the **Users & Permissions** page.
Repeated failures are visible to admins in the **Activity Log**.

**A menu entry is missing.**
Your role does not have access to that page. See
[Part 4.1](#41-who-can-do-what). If you believe you need it, ask a System Admin
to review your role.

**I get "You do not have permission to access this page".**
Same reason. Nothing is broken.

**My search returns nothing.**
Widen it — search on fewer fields, or use part of a name instead of the whole
one. On **Case Number Search**, try the case number alone. Remember that
searches only cover records you are allowed to see.

**The page looks stale or is behaving oddly.**
Refresh the browser first. If that does not help and you are a System Admin, use
**Reset Client / Clear Local App Data** — it is safe and affects only your own
device.

**My upload was refused.**
Check the file type (PDF, DOCX, JPG, PNG only) and the size (25 MB maximum).

**I cannot see Approve / Reject on a document.**
Only System Admins and Lawyers can review documents. If you are one and still
do not see the buttons, check the document is not already in that state — an
approved document shows only **Reject**, and a rejected one only **Approve**.

**I approved a document by mistake.**
Press **Reject** on it (or **Approve** again if you rejected it wrongly) — a
decision can be changed, and the uploader is notified of the correction. A
document cannot be returned to **Pending Review**; if it needs a fresh look, ask
for the file to be uploaded again.

**Pending Documents never goes down.**
Someone with review rights needs to work through them — see
[Reviewing a document](#reviewing-a-document--approve-and-reject).

**A client says they cannot upload.**
That is the default. Use **Grant Temporary Upload Access** in the **Document
Center** to open a time-limited window for one specific case.

**I keep getting Smart Follow-up nudges.**
A task assigned to you is still open. Open **Notifications** → **Reminders &
Follow-ups** and press
**Mark Done** or **Dismiss**. The nudges stop immediately.

**A case timeline shows only one step.**
That is expected. Timeline steps are not created by changing the stage, and
there is no button to add them by hand — see
[Case Timeline](#case-timeline--what-it-does-and-does-not-do). Use **Notes** to
record progress.

**The times look wrong.**
All times are Kuwait local time (UTC+3) in 12-hour AM/PM format. If a time still
looks wrong, check your own device clock and time zone.

**I was asked for a status update but the case has not moved.**
Reply anyway — do not leave it open, or automatic nudges go to you and to the
person who asked. Add a **Note** on the case explaining the position, then press
**Dismiss** on the task. Do **not** press **Mark Done**: on a status-update
request that names a requested stage, Mark Done moves the case to that stage.

**Printing came out with the menus and buttons on it.**
It should not — the system builds a separate clean sheet rather than printing
the screen (see [1.7](#17-printing)). If you used the browser's own **Ctrl+P**
instead of the page's **Print** button, you will still get a clean sheet, but
only if a sheet has been prepared; press the page's **Print** button.

**The Print button says there is nothing to print.**
The page is empty — no tasks match your filter, no documents have been
uploaded, or you have not run a search yet. Clear the filter or run the search
first.

**A hearing warning arrived on a Saturday.**
That is correct. **Saturday is a working day**; **Friday is the only weekend
day** (see [1.5 — The working week](#the-working-week)). Only a Friday warning
gets moved, and it moves **earlier**, to the Thursday.

## 5.2 Keeping the system safe

- **Never share your password.** Every action is logged under the account that
  performed it, so anything done with your credentials is recorded as done by
  you.
- **Always sign out** on shared or public computers.
- **Deactivate, do not delete**, when someone leaves the firm — it blocks
  sign-in while keeping their history attributable.
- **Take a Full Backup before any destructive operation**, and store the file
  encrypted and off the server.
- **Treat backup files as confidential.** A backup ZIP contains the firm's
  entire dataset.
- **Report anything unexpected** to your System Admin — an unfamiliar entry in
  the Activity Log, a case you did not create, a notification about a case you
  have nothing to do with.
